TL;DR

SPHERE frames MoE plasticity loss as spectral collapse in the empirical NTK, then keeps the update geometry better conditioned by regularizing weighted expert features.

Mechanism-first RL regularization
Problem Continual RL can make later-task updates effectively low-dimensional.
Method A tractable Parseval penalty on weighted expert features.
Effect Higher spectral plasticity and stronger continual-control performance.
Spectral Plasticity and SPHERE
What Defined as the effective rank of the eNTK matrix. Higher means a more isotropic spectrum that stretches update directions more uniformly.
Why When the spectrum collapses, updates become effectively low-dimensional, so new experience can’t change the policy meaningfully.
How SPHERE regularizes weighted expert features to prevent spectral collapse and keep update directions diverse throughout training.

The visualization below turns this view into a concrete geometry. Think of a unit sphere of possible gradient directions: multiplying by an eNTK matrix $\mathbf{K}$ maps the sphere into an ellipsoid. If $\mathbf{K}$ becomes low-rank, one axis shrinks toward zero and the ellipsoid degenerates into a near-plane/line. SPHERE keeps the spectrum more isotropic.

Top row ($\nabla_f L$) shows the input sphere of directions; bottom row ($\mathbf{K}\nabla_f L$) shows the stretching process.

MetaWorld · HumanoidBench
The Ubiquity of Spectral Plasticity Loss
Across architectures, performance degrades under continual RL (CRL) compared to per-task RL.
During CRL, $r_e(\mathbf{K})$ drops across baselines; SPHERE maintains higher spectral plasticity.
Mechanism: Increasing $r_e(\mathbf{K})$ Preserves Spectral Plasticity
Effective rank of the weighted expert feature Gram correlates with effective rank of the eNTK, motivating feature-Gram regularization.
Qualitative Analysis
Qualitative analysis. Using a fixed held-out batch of states from a Stair-task replay buffer, we run t-SNE on last-layer gating-weighted expert features from checkpoints after each task. Each point is colored by the dominant singular-direction index of the weighted feature matrix (largest squared projection). Without SPHERE, points quickly concentrate on a single dominant component; with SPHERE, multiple components remain active throughout the sequence.
